# What Is GEO Scoring?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) measures whether a web page is likely
to be read, understood, and cited by AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity,
Google AI Overviews, and similar systems — when they answer a user's
question.

A GEO analysis scores a page against 18 criteria, each worth 0-10 points.
Non-local businesses are scored out of a maximum of 150 (15 applicable
criteria). Local businesses are scored out of 180, since three additional
local-specific criteria apply to them.

## Scoring Bands

- **150-180: Strong GEO presence** — the page is well optimized for AI
  citation.
- **110-149: Moderate — fixable gaps** — solid foundation, but specific
  gaps are holding the page back.
- **60-109: Weak — significant work needed** — multiple core criteria are
  underperforming.
- **0-59: Invisible to AI engines** — the page is very unlikely to be
  cited or referenced by AI systems in its current state.

## How Criteria Are Scored

Each of the 18 criteria is scored on the same 0-10 scale:

- **0-3:** The signal is absent, vague, or unclear.
- **4-6:** The signal exists but is incomplete, generic, or inconsistently
  applied.
- **7-10:** The signal is specific, complete, and clearly present.

Three criteria — Local Entity Signals, Hyper-Local Content, and Local
Citation Signals — automatically score 5/10 for non-local businesses,
since they don't meaningfully apply outside a local-business context.

## Who Fixes What: fix_owner

Every recommended fix is labeled with who can actually implement it:

- **"site owner"** — anyone managing the website's content or code can do
  this (rewriting copy, adding a heading, restructuring a section).
- **"business owner"** — requires the actual business behind the site
  (claiming a Google Business Profile, gathering reviews, providing
  credentials or certifications).

This distinction matters because some fixes are quick content edits,
while others require the business itself to take action outside the
website.

## How the Analysis Works

1. The page's content is fetched and cleaned (navigation clutter removed,
   trimmed to a working length, cut cleanly at a sentence boundary).
2. The cleaned content is analyzed against all 18 criteria, using specific
   sentences and sections from the actual page — never generic feedback.
3. Every criterion gets a score, a reason grounded in the actual page
   content, a specific fix, and a fix_owner label.
4. The top 3 highest-impact, lowest-effort fixes are highlighted
   separately as "Quick Wins."
