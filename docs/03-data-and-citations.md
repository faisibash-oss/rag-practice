# Data & Citation Signals Criteria

These three criteria measure whether a page contains the kind of
specific, quotable, structurally-recognizable content AI engines look
for when selecting what to cite.

## Stat Density (0-10)

How many specific numbers, percentages, measurements, timeframes, or
data points appear on the page?

- 0-3: No specific numbers. Only vague claims like "many" or "most."
- 4-6: Some numbers present but without context or sourcing.
- 7-10: Multiple specific, contextualized statistics distributed
  throughout the page.

## Citation-Worthy Statements (0-10)

Does the page contain short, precise, standalone facts that an AI would
want to quote directly?

- 0-3: All statements are vague or promotional.
- 4-6: Some specific statements exist but are not precisely or quotably
  worded.
- 7-10: Multiple precise, self-contained, quotable facts are present.

## Schema Signals (0-10)

Does the visible content suggest structured data — FAQ sections, article
dates, organization details, author bylines, review markup? This is
scored based on visible content only, not HTML source code — a page can
score well here even without technical schema markup, if the visible
content structure implies it (e.g., a clearly formatted FAQ section
implies FAQ schema potential).

- 0-3: No structured content signals visible.
- 4-6: Some structural elements present but incomplete.
- 7-10: Clear content structure that maps to FAQ, Article, or
  Organization schema types.
