# Trust & Credibility Criteria

These five criteria measure whether a page establishes clear, credible
identity and expertise signals — the things that make an AI engine
confident enough in a source to cite it.

## Entity Clarity (0-10)

Is the brand or business explicitly defined? Name, what it does, who it
serves, and where it operates must be stated — not assumed.

- 0-3: Brand identity is absent or vague.
- 4-6: Partially defined. One or more key details are missing.
- 7-10: Complete entity definition is present and unambiguous.

## Source Attribution (0-10)

Are claims backed by named, credible sources?

- 0-3: No sources cited. Anonymous language like "studies show" or
  "experts say."
- 4-6: Some attribution present but vague or low-credibility.
- 7-10: Multiple named, credible sources with specific references.

## Expertise Signals (0-10)

Are there author credentials, certifications, years of experience, named
professionals, or verifiable expertise indicators?

- 0-3: No expertise signals. Content is anonymous.
- 4-6: Some signals present but vague or unverifiable.
- 7-10: Specific, named, verifiable expertise indicators clearly
  present.

## Brand Consistency (0-10)

Is the brand name used clearly and consistently — never abbreviated,
varied, or ambiguous?

- 0-3: Brand name absent, inconsistent, or confusingly varied.
- 4-6: Mostly consistent with minor gaps.
- 7-10: Brand name clear and consistent throughout all content sections.

## Content Freshness (0-10)

Are there visible dates, current year references, recent statistics, or
update timestamps?

- 0-3: No dates visible. Content feels static or outdated.
- 4-6: Some dated content but recency is not clearly signaled.
- 7-10: Clear recency signals throughout — current year stats, visible
  update dates.
